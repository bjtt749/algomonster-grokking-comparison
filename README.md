## First, fix the "Grokking" confusion

There are two legitimate places to buy Grokking the Coding Interview, and they are not the same product experience.

**Design Gurus** is where the original authors publish. You buy either lifetime access to the individual course or an annual all-courses subscription, and you get the course plus a certification option and mock interview services sold separately. The course page currently advertises lifetime access at **$197** and full access to all 245 problems. Design Gurus runs regional pricing and sales, so the number you see varies by country.

**Educative** licenses the Grokking courses into a much bigger subscription. You don't buy Grokking there; you buy Educative. The interview prep tier currently shows Standard at **$11/month billed annually ($120 for the year, listed down from $245)**, Premium at **$14/month billed annually ($161, from $358)**, and Premium Plus at **$17/month billed annually ($201, from $479)**. Monthly billing exists at around $59/month. One practical catch worth knowing before you sign up: interview prep content, Grokking included, is excluded from Educative's free trial, so you can't evaluate the actual course for free.

That's the whole reason "algomonster vs grokking" has no clean answer on price alone. One side is a focused product with two plans. The other side is a course available through two vendors with at least four purchase paths.

## What AlgoMonster actually is

AlgoMonster is built by ex-Google engineers and organized around patterns rather than a problem feed. The teaching format is text and diagrams, not video: a pattern gets explained, a template gets shown, then you work problems that belong to that pattern. There's a Speedrun mode for timed review, an in-browser editor, and an AI assistant for questions and debugging.

Two things about it are worth calling out because they're differentiators rather than marketing copy.

The first is the decision flowcharts. Knowing sliding window exists is easy; deciding that a problem is a sliding window problem under interview pressure is the actual skill being tested. Content organized around "which pattern is this?" addresses that directly.

The second is the business model. There's no monthly plan. Your options are a yearly subscription or a one-time lifetime payment, which means no recurring charge silently renewing while you're eight months into a job you already landed.

Sizing is where sources disagree, and it's worth being honest about it rather than quoting one number. Reviews of the standalone DSA product commonly cite 48 patterns and roughly 230 lessons. The current checkout page advertises **500+ lessons across 6 courses, 1,000+ illustrations and 3,000+ editorials**, plus bonus courses including a CS foundation course and a build-an-LLM course. The lesson and editorial counts reflect the expanded course bundle, not just the DSA track. Either way you're buying a curated pattern library, not a 3,000-problem LeetCode replacement.

## How you actually study on each platform

Both are text-first, which matters more than people expect. Text is searchable, skimmable, and fast to review the night before an interview. Video walkthroughs are better when you're learning something completely new and need someone to narrate the thinking.

Where they diverge is in what surrounds the lesson. AlgoMonster's Speedrun mode and decision flowcharts are built for pre-interview review, and its AI assistant sits inside the practice environment for debugging help. Educative brings AI mock interviews on the Premium tiers, plus its playground environment and API courses, IT courses, and the rest of a general-purpose library that has nothing to do with interviews.

The Educative trade-off is inversely the Design Gurus trade-off. Educative gives you volume: a coding course, a system design course, and a library you'll mostly never open. Design Gurus gives you the same coding course, lifetime, without the library attached.

## System design is the one genuinely decisive difference

If you're interviewing at senior level or above, the loop includes a design round, and this is where the comparison stops being close.

Grokking's system design course is widely treated as one of the strongest available, and it's included in Educative's subscription. AlgoMonster's system design coverage is introductory: load balancing, caching, database sharding and fundamentals. Enough to know the vocabulary, not enough to run a 45-minute design discussion about a news feed or a rate limiter at scale. Its own reviews describe it as a DSA-first product.

So if you need design prep, price AlgoMonster plus a separate system design resource, not AlgoMonster alone. That changes the math considerably, and it's the honest version of the "which is cheaper" question.

AlgoMonster runs sales regularly, and the prices shown on its subscribe page are already promotional rather than list. On the Educative side, plan prices fluctuate with frequent promotions too, and students with a valid .edu address qualify for additional discounts on some tiers. Design Gurus also applies regional pricing, which is why you'll see everything from roughly $180 to over $370 quoted for its yearly subscription depending on which blog you read and where that blog's readers are located.

The practical advice: check the checkout page rather than the coupon blog. Promotional pricing on both platforms changes faster than roundups get updated, and a dead code is worse than no code because it wastes a checkout attempt.

## FAQ

**Is Grokking the Coding Interview still worth it?**
The pattern method holds up. What has changed is the pricing landscape: you can now buy an equivalent pattern curriculum for less, and the Grokking brand is split across two vendors, which makes direct price comparison awkward. If you specifically want the original, Design Gurus lifetime access at $197 is a reasonable one-time purchase.

**How many patterns do I actually need?**
For most US tech interviews, somewhere between 15 and 20 pattern families cover the overwhelming majority of medium and hard problems. Anything beyond that is refinement, not new territory.

**Does AlgoMonster have a free trial?**
There's a free tier with limited access to sample patterns and problems. It's enough to judge whether the text-and-diagram format works for you, and not enough to prepare for interviews on its own.

**Can I pay for AlgoMonster monthly?**
No. The plans are yearly or lifetime. If you need a short, cheap window, that's a real constraint rather than a detail.

**Is the lifetime plan worth it for a single job search?**
Only if your search runs long or you expect to interview again within a few years. If you're confident you'll finish in six weeks and then not open the platform again for three years, the yearly plan is the better financial call, and it's the same content.

**Do I need both AlgoMonster and Grokking?**
Almost certainly not for coding rounds. The pattern coverage overlaps heavily. If you want a second resource, put the money into system design or mock interviews instead, where the gap in your prep is usually larger.
